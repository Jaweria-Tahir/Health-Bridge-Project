Vaccination - General Guidance
Vaccines help the immune system recognize and fight specific diseases before infection
causes serious illness. Childhood vaccination schedules typically cover diseases such as
measles, polio, hepatitis B, and tetanus, given in a series of doses starting from birth
through early childhood. Adults may need booster doses (such as tetanus boosters every
10 years) and seasonal vaccines like influenza. Common mild side effects include soreness
at the injection site, low fever, and tiredness for a day or two. Anyone with vaccine
allergies or ongoing health conditions should consult a licensed healthcare provider
before vaccination, and local vaccination centers can provide official schedules.
